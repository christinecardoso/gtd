It depends on your branding strategy and how distinct these aspects of your business are. Here are some things to consider:

### **Option 1: Keep Everything Under One Brand (Best for Personal Brands & Small Businesses)**

If your blog, classes, and bread sales are all related and appeal to the same audience, keeping them under one Instagram/TikTok/website can be beneficial. It allows you to:  
✔️ **Build a strong, recognizable brand** – Everything falls under your expertise.  
✔️ **Cross-promote easily** – Blog readers may want to take a class, and class students may want to buy your bread.  
✔️ **Save time & effort** – Managing one account is easier than juggling multiple.

🔹 **Best Approach:** Have a clear bio (e.g., “Baking expert | Online classes | Fresh sourdough delivered”) and use highlights or separate posts to differentiate each aspect.

### **Option 2: Separate Channels for Different Audiences (Best for Scaling a Business)**

If your audiences are very different (e.g., blog readers are hobby bakers, class takers are serious learners, and bread buyers just want to eat great bread), separate accounts might work better.  
✔️ **More targeted content** – You can speak directly to each audience without confusing them.  
✔️ **Better branding for sales** – If you want your bread sales to grow into a standalone business, a dedicated account can make it look more professional.  
✔️ **Easier to scale** – If you want to expand one aspect (like selling more bread), you won’t overwhelm your blog readers.

🔹 **Best Approach:**

- **@YourBreadBlog** → Blog, recipes, community engagement
    
- **@YourBreadClasses** → Baking workshops, behind-the-scenes, testimonials
    
- **@YourBreadShop** → Professional, polished content focused on selling
    

### **Hybrid Approach (A Middle Ground)**

Many businesses start with one channel and branch out as they grow. You can:  
✅ Keep one main page but create separate landing pages on your website for classes/sales.  
✅ Use Instagram highlights or pinned posts to make it easy to navigate between blog, classes, and sales.  
✅ If one aspect (like selling bread) grows a lot, create a separate business page later.

### **Final Thoughts**

If you’re just starting out, I’d recommend keeping everything under **one strong brand** and seeing how your audience reacts. If one area starts to dominate, **then consider separating it**. The key is making sure your messaging is **clear and easy to navigate** for your followers. 🚀

Breaking the email into **"In Scope"** and **"Out of Scope"** sections can make it more **clear and easy to follow** for the client. This approach helps set clear boundaries and expectations for what is included in the budget and what additional services may be required for extra costs. It also helps prevent any potential misunderstandings about the services you will provide and any limitations.

Here’s how you could structure the email for better clarity:

### **In Scope**:

- **Two high-quality blog posts per month** (researched, written, and optimized)

### **Out of Scope**:

- **Performance Tracking & Reporting**: Performance reports and analytics are not included at this budget. If you would like detailed KPI tracking and reporting, this would require an adjustment to the budget.

- **Content Scheduling & Publishing**: If you'd like me to handle the actual scheduling and publishing of posts, this would be an additional service. Alternatively, if you prefer to manage this in-house, I can provide the content and suggestions for timing.
    
- **Additional Meetings**: If more than **one weekly meeting** is required, additional meetings can be scheduled and billed at an hourly rate.
    
- **Additional Revisions**: If the content requires significant revisions or changes beyond the initial approval round, this would be subject to additional charges.
    

---

Please let me know if this breakdown aligns with your expectations or if you’d like to adjust anything. I’d be happy to schedule a call next week to discuss the details further and tailor the proposal to your specific needs.

Looking forward to hearing from you!

Best,  
Christine

### **Why This Works:**

1. **Clarity**: Separating "In Scope" and "Out of Scope" gives the client an organized view of what’s included, making the proposal easier to digest.
    
2. **Prevents Misunderstanding**: It helps avoid the potential issue of the client assuming something is included that isn't part of the agreement.
    
3. **Sets Boundaries**: It clearly defines the limitations and extra costs for services outside the agreed scope, reducing any scope creep.
    

This structure keeps things transparent and organized, ensuring both parties understand the boundaries of the service you're offering.

## Why It's Out of Scope
Yes, it makes sense that these items would be considered **out of budget** for the $2,000 per month proposal, as they typically involve additional time, resources, and services that go beyond the core content creation and strategy you're offering. Here’s why they make sense as out-of-budget items:

### **1. Performance Tracking & Reporting**

- **Why it's out of scope**: In-depth performance tracking and reporting require ongoing data analysis, possibly using specialized tools or software. These services often need additional time and expertise to produce detailed reports with actionable insights, making them an extra cost.
    

### **2. Paid Promotion**

- **Why it's out of scope**: Paid campaigns involve budget allocation for ads, management of the campaigns, and advanced targeting. This service requires a separate budget, as it could involve daily monitoring, reporting, optimization, and testing of ads or paid content.
    

### **3. Content Scheduling & Publishing**

- **Why it's out of scope**: Scheduling and publishing posts on various platforms can take up significant time, especially if you need to monitor specific time frames, ensure posts go live at optimal times, and handle any technical issues. It may also require using subscription-based tools for scheduling, which could add to the overall cost.
    

### **4. Additional Meetings**

- **Why it's out of scope**: Weekly meetings are included, but if the client requests more meetings (for strategy sessions, content reviews, or other purposes), it would require additional time, making it a chargeable service.
    

### **5. Additional Revisions**

- **Why it's out of scope**: Significant revisions beyond the initial round of feedback can often take more time and may involve rewriting content, adjusting strategy, or producing new assets. For the given budget, a set number of revisions is expected to keep things on track without going over the agreed-upon time allocation.
    

These services would be **additional costs** because they require extra work and resources. By clarifying that they’re outside of the $2,000/month scope, you are setting clear boundaries and ensuring that both you and the client have realistic expectations regarding what’s included in the initial budget.

If your Bola de Água came out too dense, there are a few possible reasons and adjustments you can try for a lighter, airier texture:

1️⃣ Not Enough Hydration

• These rolls should have a relatively high water content for a chewy yet light texture.

✅ Fix: Try increasing the water slightly (by 5-10%) if the dough felt stiff or dry.

  

2️⃣ Under-Kneading

• Gluten needs proper development to trap air and create an open crumb.

✅ Fix: Knead the dough well until it passes the windowpane test (stretch a small piece thin—if it tears too easily, keep kneading).

  

3️⃣ Not Enough Proofing Time

• If the dough didn’t rise enough, the rolls won’t have the proper lightness.

✅ Fix: Allow for a full first rise (it should double in size). If it’s cold where you are, extend proofing time.

  

4️⃣ Overproofing or Underproofing Before Baking

• Overproofed dough can collapse; underproofed dough won’t have enough air inside.

✅ Fix: After shaping, let the rolls rise until puffy but not deflated (a light poke should leave an indentation that slowly springs back).

  

5️⃣ Oven Temperature Too Low

• A hot oven helps create oven spring, which gives the rolls their airy structure.

✅ Fix: Make sure your oven is preheated properly—aim for 450°F (230°C) and use steam (a tray of hot water or misting the oven).

  

6️⃣ Wrong Flour Type

• If using a low-protein flour, the dough won’t develop enough structure.

✅ Fix: Use bread flour (higher protein) instead of all-purpose for better elasticity and chew.



Makes: 8 rolls

Time: About 3 hours (including rising and baking time)


Ingredients:

• 500g (4 cups) bread flour

• 7g (2¼ tsp) active dry yeast (or 6g instant yeast)

• 10g (2 tsp) salt

• 5g (1 tsp) sugar

• 300ml (1¼ cups) warm water (not hot, around 38°C/100°F)

• 15ml (1 tbsp) olive oil

  

  

  

## Instructions:

  

1️⃣ Activate the Yeast (If Using Active Dry Yeast)

• In a small bowl, mix 300ml (1¼ cups) warm water with 5g (1 tsp) sugar and 7g (2¼ tsp) active dry yeast.

• Let sit for 5–10 minutes until frothy. (If using instant yeast, skip this step and mix the yeast directly with the flour.)

  

2️⃣ Mix & Knead the Dough

• In a large mixing bowl, combine 500g (4 cups) bread flour and 10g (2 tsp) salt.

• Pour in the yeast mixture (or dry instant yeast) and add 15ml (1 tbsp) olive oil.

• Mix until a rough dough forms, then knead on a floured surface for 10–12 minutes (or 7–8 minutes in a stand mixer with a dough hook).

• The dough should be smooth, elastic, and slightly tacky.

  

3️⃣ First Rise (Bulk Fermentation)

• Place the dough in a lightly greased bowl, cover with a damp towel, and let it rise in a warm place for 1½ to 2 hours, or until doubled in size.

  

4️⃣ Shape the Rolls

• Punch down the dough and divide it into 8 equal pieces (about 100g each).

• Shape each into an oval, flatten slightly, then fold lengthwise like a taco, pinching the seam.

• Place rolls seam-side down on a lightly floured baking sheet.

  

5️⃣ Second Rise

• Cover the rolls loosely and let rise for 30–45 minutes, until puffy but not overproofed.

  

6️⃣ Score & Bake

• Preheat oven to 230°C (450°F).

• Right before baking, use a sharp knife or bench scraper to make a deep crease down the center of each roll.

• Mist the oven with water (or place a tray of hot water inside) to create steam, which helps form a crisp crust.

• Bake for 15–18 minutes, until golden brown and crusty.

  

7️⃣ Cool & Enjoy

• Let the rolls cool on a wire rack for at least 10 minutes before eating.

• Serve fresh with butter, cheese, or use them for classic Portuguese bifana sandwiches!

   

🔹 Pro Tips for Light & Airy Papo Secos:

✅ Use bread flour for the best chewiness.

✅ Don’t rush the first rise—it helps develop flavor and structure.

✅ Steam in the oven makes the crust light and crispy.

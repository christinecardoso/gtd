
Thanks for sharing your perspective. We all agree that issues like drug use, homelessness, and safety require real solutions. But giving away public land isn’t a solution—it’s a deflection.

  

This isn’t about political ideology. It’s about protecting public assets that belong to all residents, not just those who live next to them. That “tiny strip” has been used for over 60 years—by families fishing, people walking dogs, neighbors launching paddleboards, and yes, people quietly enjoying the water. The idea that only large parks matter ignores the value of small, local access points that serve as daily mental health and community anchors.

  

Giving the land away rewards city inaction and sets a precedent that public land can be transferred to private hands whenever things get hard. What’s next? Bryant Park? If we want safety and cleanliness, we should push for better maintenance, patrols, and community solutions—not the removal of access.

  

We can address crime and homelessness without sacrificing shared spaces that make this city livable for all, not just a few. Let’s expect more from our city—not less.
